Natural History Museum’s ‘living laboratory’ logs 11 million environmental records in first year
<p>The Natural History Museum’s (NHM’s) “living laboratory” garden has collected more than 11 million <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/resources/Infrastructure-as-a-Service-IaaS">environmental data records</a> in its first year, feeding readings from more than 50 sensors into the Museum’s Amazon Web Services (AWS)-powered Data Ecosystem.</p> <p>The sensors in the Nature Discovery Garden continuously capture temperature and humidity to track microclimate variation, underwater acoustic recordings from the pond, birdsong and ambient urban noise such as traffic.</p> <p>During June’s heatwave, soil sensors in the garden’s woodland areas recorded temperatures 10°C cooler than the paved areas of the Darwin Centre Courtyard, giving researchers evidence for how trees, shade, soil moisture and permeable surfaces can help cities adapt to rising temperatures.</p> <p>The readings feed a Data Ecosystem built on AWS cloud technologies, which collects, processes and shares millions of observations from sensors, imagery and other scientific sources. In practice, that means an <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/Natural-History-Museum-deploys-sensor-network-to-decode-urban-biodiversity-with-AWS">internet of things (IoT) data pipeline</a> in which devices publish telemetry to a message broker such as AWS IoT Core, streamed through a service such as Kinesis into an <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/Storage-technology-explained-What-is-S3-and-what-is-it-good-for">S3</a>-based <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/Cloud-data-lakes-Where-do-they-fit-and-what-are-their-benefits">data lake</a>, then processed, catalogued and analysed with tools such as Athena and SageMaker – including machine learning models that classify birdsong and other acoustic recordings.</p> <p>Ed Baker (pictured), acoustic biology researcher at the Natural History Museum, said: “We call the Museum gardens a living laboratory, and the sensor network and AWS Data Ecosystem have made this a reality. By collecting millions of data points, from temperature and humidity to birdsong and underwater sound, we can build a far richer picture of how urban nature responds to various pressures and becomes more resilient to future environmental challenges.”</p> <p>The museum is now expanding the network into its Evolution Garden, an area with higher visitor footfall and a contrasting planting style, to study how visitor activity and habitat design influence biodiversity.</p> <p>Long-term monitoring since 1995 has recorded more than 3,500 species in the gardens, with a further 90 identified since they reopened in 2024, including the emperor moth and the small red-eyed damselfly.</p> <div class="extra-info"> <div class="extra-info-inner"> <h3 class="splash-heading">Read more about cloud and sustainability</h3> <ul class="default-list"> <li><a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/feature/Natural-History-Museum-deploys-sensor-network-to-decode-urban-biodiversity-with-AWS">Natural History Museum deploys sensor network to decode urban biodiversity with AWS</a>: We look at how the Museum’s sensor network and AWS Data Ecosystem turn a London garden into a living laboratory for urban biodiversity research.</li> <li><a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366644373/Data-dive-Dodgy-data-derails-datacentre-water-debate">Data dive: Dodgy data derails datacentre water debate</a>: We investigate how poor-quality data undermines claims about datacentre water consumption.</li> </ul> </div> </div> <p>The project is part of a wider pattern of deployments of environmental sensor networks allied to cloud data platforms. <a href="https://www.technologyreview.com/2022/08/19/1057848/array-of-things-goes-global/">In Chicago, the Array of Things</a> deployed hundreds of urban sensing nodes publishing open data through a cloud portal; in Germany, the <a href="https://www.nature4.org/">Ammod automated multisensor stations and the Nature 4.0 project</a> combine networked sensors with machine learning for biodiversity monitoring.
