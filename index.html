Government science advisers: ‘No framework for datacentre demand’
<p>The UK has no agreed method for working out how much onshore datacentre capacity it needs, and delays in securing grid connections are a key factor in where new sites go.</p> <p>That’s according to the <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/resources/IT-for-government-and-public-sector">Government Office for Science</a>, which published a report of a meeting chaired by chief scientific adviser Angela McLean in February. <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-centre-siting-in-the-uk/data-centre-siting-in-the-uk">The report</a> represents “the combined views of roundtable participants” and is explicitly “not a statement of government policy” but does provide useful insight into the datacentre debate at national planning level.</p> <p>According to the report, the group found “no obvious frameworks to determine how much onshore datacentre capacity the UK will need in the future”, and said demand was “very hard to estimate”. Sizing decisions should start from what UK capacity is essential – where offshore provision is not an option, it said – and from tasks datacentres perform across the economy, rather than from sector-by-sector demand.</p> <p>“Water is not a major constraint on datacentre siting in the UK,” it said. The notes state that datacentres can be cooled without water using closed-loop systems and ventilation, given the UK’s climate, and that Amazon Web Services’ facilities reportedly run waterless for around 97% of the year.
