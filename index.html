Driven by the demand for artificial intelligence (AI) compute, and in the context of the government’s decision in 2024 to <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366611074/UK-government-adds-datacentres-to-CNI-regime-Why-did-it-take-so-long">classify datacentres as critical national infrastructure</a>, developers are racing to secure planning consent, grid connections and power across the country.</p> <p>New analysis of Barbour ABI project data, compiled for Computer Weekly, shows that the <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/resources/Data-Centre">committed datacentre pipeline</a> – projects that have entered the planning system but have not yet completed – now stands at 173 projects and capacity of more than 14GW. That compares with a <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366640447/Hit-the-north-UK-datacentre-focus-shifts-to-M62-and-points-north">March snapshot</a> of 114 projects and roughly 10GW, a rise of nearly 60 projects and about 4.5GW in six months.</p> <p>The headline capacity of 14.6GW sits on top of a further 4.4GW of “pre-planning” projects – schemes that have been publicly floated but have not yet submitted a planning application.
